\begin{abstract}
Environmental Information Disclosure (EID) is becoming a macroeconomic
data element in China's transition toward new quality productive forces
and in the 15th Five-Year Plan (2026--2030). This paper develops a dynamic
green portfolio framework that embeds database-reported EID scores
directly into a Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) state space and
evaluates whether this information is \emph{associated with} improved
neural-network structural stability. Using 59,051 firm-year EID
observations for Chinese listed companies from 2008 to 2025 and
out-of-sample portfolio tests for 2022--2024, the PPO+EID+Regime model
reduces normalized Frobenius parameter distance by 36--50\% relative
to baseline PPO (bootstrap 95\% CI: [29\%, 57\%]; $p < 0.001$). On a
\emph{net-return} basis with proportional costs of 0.2\% per turnover unit,
the model raises the Sharpe ratio from 0.29 (Markowitz) to 0.58 and
lowers maximum drawdown from $-35.2\%$ to $-21.3\%$. A mediation
analysis provides evidence consistent with the hypothesized
mechanism---that EID reduces policy-risk noise in training rewards,
thereby stabilizing policy gradients---but does not constitute a formal
causal proof. The evidence suggests that EID serves as a policy-risk
signal that improves stability of learned portfolio policies, though
causal attribution and generalizability require additional validation.
\textbf{Reproducibility}: Code and data construction scripts are
available at \url{https://github.com/wangweijia462/-}.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Green Finance, Environmental Information
Disclosure, Proximal Policy Optimization, Deep Reinforcement Learning,
Neural Network Stability, Portfolio Optimization, China
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

China's shift from factor-driven growth to new quality productive forces
turns firm-level data into infrastructure for macroeconomic risk
governance. In the 15th Five-Year Plan (2026--2030), environmental
governance, digital transformation, and industrial upgrading jointly
require capital markets to identify firms that can adapt to binding
resource and carbon constraints. Environmental Information Disclosure
(EID) therefore operates at two levels: at the firm level it records
environmental governance and compliance capacity; at the macro level it
may function as a data element helping investors identify enterprises
able to support high-quality development while reducing systemic risk
during the green transition.

Existing dynamic portfolio optimization frameworks face methodological
barriers when integrating EID. Traditional mean-variance approaches
\citep{markowitz1952portfolio} assume known distributions and cannot
accommodate time-varying policy risk. Emerging deep reinforcement
learning (DRL) methods restrict state spaces to market microstructure
variables \citep{mnih2013playing,schulman2017proximal}, systematically
excluding firm-level environmental metrics. This exclusion may sacrifice
information about fundamental stability during policy regime transitions.

\paragraph{Research question.} Can a portfolio policy that observes EID
improve allocation while exhibiting greater structural stability across
market regimes, relative to one that does not? We distinguish between
two related but distinct questions: (1) whether EID improves
out-of-sample performance; and (2) whether EID reduces
\emph{parameter instability}---measured by normalized Frobenius distance
between independently trained networks---as a sign that the model has
captured persistent economic information rather than period-specific
patterns. This paper focuses primarily on (2), while providing
supporting evidence on (1).

\paragraph{A note on causal language.} The central claim of this paper
is \emph{empirical and associational}: models trained with EID exhibit
lower parameter distance across training subsets. The hypothesized
causal chain (EID $\to$ lower reward noise $\to$ lower gradient variance
$\to$ lower Frobenius distance) is theoretically motivated and
consistent with the data, but the paper does not possess the
identification conditions needed for a formal causal proof. We use
``associated with'' and ``consistent with'' rather than ``causes''
throughout.

\paragraph{Marginal contributions.}
\textbf{Contribution 1.} We develop a formally stated hypothesis that
EID reduces policy-risk noise in DRL training and propose the
normalized Frobenius distance as a testable proxy for gradient
stability, with bootstrap confidence intervals and permutation tests.

\textbf{Contribution 2.} We provide a mediation decomposition that
quantifies how much of the EID-stability association is accounted for
by reward variance reduction---the hypothesized intermediate
variable---versus direct parameter effects.

\textbf{Contribution 3.} We deliver a full net-return comparison across
multiple cost levels (0.1--0.3\% per turnover unit) against six
benchmarks including equal-weight, Markowitz minimum variance, momentum,
risk parity, a pure-DRL baseline, and a static ESG tilt.

\paragraph{Paper structure.}
Section~\ref{sec:methodology} develops the MDP formulation, PPO
objective, EID reward design, and Frobenius stability metric with formal
justification. Section~\ref{sec:data} describes data sources and
integrity rules. Section~\ref{sec:results} presents empirical results
organized by primary finding (stability) and secondary validation
(performance). Section~\ref{sec:robustness} reports robustness and
out-of-distribution tests. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses
policy implications and limitations. Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}
concludes.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Economic Logic, State Space, and Reward Design}

The proposed framework $\mathcal{F}:\{S,R,P,E\}\to\pi^*(a|s)$
integrates four modules: state construction $S$, reward design $R$, PPO
training $P$, and stability evaluation $E$.

\subsubsection{Theoretical Motivation: EID as Policy-Risk Signal}

\begin{theorem}[EID as Modified Markov Transition Probability]
\label{thm:eid_mdp}
Let $P(\mathrm{PolicyShock}_t\mid e_{it})=\alpha_0-\alpha_1 e_{it}+\varepsilon_t$
with $\alpha_1>0$, so that higher EID reduces policy-shock probability.
In the MDP framework this modifies the state transition distribution.
If the state includes $e_{it}$ and EID genuinely affects future returns
through the downside-risk channel,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial V}{\partial e_{it}}
  = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{\tau=0}^{\infty}\gamma^\tau
    \frac{\partial r_{t+\tau}}{\partial e_{it}}\,\Big|\,s_t\right]>0,
  \label{eq:value_gradient}
\end{equation}
where the positive sign operates through lower partial moments:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial\,\mathrm{LPM}_{q,i}(\tau\mid e_{it})}{\partial e_{it}}<0,
  \quad q\geq 1.
  \label{eq:lpm}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
Theorem~\ref{thm:eid_mdp} formalizes a theoretical condition under which
EID improves the information content of the state space. Whether the
empirical EID scores in our dataset satisfy $\alpha_1>0$ is a testable
hypothesis, not an assumption. We test this in Section~\ref{sec:mechanism_test}.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Signal mechanism and gradient variance.}
Following \citet{spence1973job}, disclosure quality is informative when
high-quality firms can credibly bear transparency costs that low-quality
firms cannot mimic \citep{verrecchia2001essays,lambert2007accounting}.
In this setting, EID reduces information asymmetry about environmental
governance. The hypothesized chain is:
\begin{equation}
\text{Higher EID} \Rightarrow \text{lower info asymmetry}
  \Rightarrow \text{lower policy-shock ambiguity}
  \Rightarrow \text{lower reward noise}
  \Rightarrow \text{lower } \mathrm{Var}(\nabla_\theta J(\theta)).
\label{eq:mechanism_chain}
\end{equation}
This chain implies a testable prediction on intermediate variables
(reward variance, gradient variance) that we examine in
Section~\ref{sec:mechanism_test}.

\begin{proposition}[Regime-Adaptive Green Preference]
\label{prop:regime}
Investor $i$ faces:
$\max_{w_t} U_t = r_p(t) - \lambda_{\mathrm{risk}}\sigma_p(t) + \lambda_2(t)\cdot f(\mathrm{Green}_t)$,
where the dynamic green preference weight satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_2(t) = \lambda_2^{\min}
    + (\lambda_2^{\max}-\lambda_2^{\min})\cdot\mathrm{Slack}_t,
  \label{eq:adaptive_lambda}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda_2^{\min}=0.05$, $\lambda_2^{\max}=0.15$, and
$\mathrm{Slack}_t=1-\mathrm{clip}(x_t,0,1)$, $x_t$ the normalized trailing
market risk premium. This inverse mapping is economically motivated:
environmental preference is largest when risk budgets are ample and
smallest when capital preservation dominates.
\end{proposition}

\subsubsection{State Space Construction}

The base state vector at time $t$ is
$S_t=\{P_t, I_t, E_t^{\mathrm{raw}}\}$, where:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $P_t=(r_{1t},\ldots,r_{Nt})$: daily returns of $N$ assets.
  \item $I_t=(\mathrm{MA}_{5,t},\mathrm{MA}_{20,t},\mathrm{RSI}_t,
    \mathrm{MACD}_t,\ldots)$: standard technical indicators.
  \item $E_t^{\mathrm{raw}}=(e_{1t}^{\mathrm{raw}},\ldots,e_{Nt}^{\mathrm{raw}})$:
    the official aggregate EID scores, carried forward from the most recent
    annual disclosure date: $e_{it}^{\mathrm{raw}}=\mathrm{EID}_i^{\mathrm{public}}(y)$,
    $t\in[t_{\mathrm{disc}}(y),t_{\mathrm{disc}}(y+1))$.
\end{itemize}

Returns are $r_{it}=(P_{i,t}-P_{i,t-1})/P_{i,t-1}$.
Volatility uses a 30-day EWMA with decay $\lambda=0.94$.
Technical indicators follow standard market implementations.
EID scores are normalized monotonically
$\tilde{e}_{it}=e_{it}^{\mathrm{raw}}/\max_{i,t\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{train}}}e_{it}^{\mathrm{raw}}$
for network conditioning, preserving rank and interval ordering.
Non-EID features use expanding-window min-max normalization during
training and 252-day rolling windows during deployment.

\subsubsection{Reward Function Design}

The EID-constrained reward function is:
\begin{equation}
  R_t = r_p(t) - \lambda_{\mathrm{risk}}\sigma_p(t)
      + \lambda_1\Delta\mathrm{SR}_t
      + \lambda_2(t)\cdot\mathrm{Green}_t,
  \label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}
where $r_p(t)=\sum_i w_{it}r_{it}$ is portfolio return,
$\sigma_p(t)=\sqrt{\sum_{i,j}w_{it}w_{jt}\mathrm{Cov}(r_{it},r_{jt})}$
is portfolio volatility, $\Delta\mathrm{SR}_t$ is the change in
trailing 252-day Sharpe ratio, $\mathrm{Green}_t=\sum_i w_{it}e_{it}^{\mathrm{raw}}$
is the weighted EID score, and $\lambda_2(t)$ follows
Proposition~\ref{prop:regime}. Hyperparameters: $\lambda_{\mathrm{risk}}=2.0$,
$\lambda_1=0.5$.

\paragraph{Transaction costs.}
One-period turnover and net return are:
\begin{align}
  \mathrm{TO}_t &= \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^N|w_{it}-w_{i,t-1}|,
  \label{eq:turnover} \\
  r_{p,t}^{\mathrm{net}} &= r_{p,t}^{\mathrm{gross}} - c\cdot\mathrm{TO}_t,
  \label{eq:net_return}
\end{align}
where $c\in\{0.001,0.002,0.003\}$ spans 0.1--0.3\% per unit of
turnover. Net returns are used in the primary performance table
(Section~\ref{sec:performance}).

\subsubsection{Market Regime Classification}

Market regime is identified using a two-state Hidden Markov Model:
\begin{equation}
  B_t = \mathbf{1}\{\mathbb{E}[r_t]>\mu_{\mathrm{LT}}\}
        \cdot\mathbf{1}\{\mathrm{SK}(r_t)>-0.5\}.
\end{equation}
The dynamic weight allocation sets $\lambda_2(t)=0.15$ in bullish
regimes and $\lambda_2(t)=0.05$ in bearish regimes, consistent with
Proposition~\ref{prop:regime}.

\subsection{PPO Training}

Both actor and critic networks use the architecture:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{Input}_{d_s}\to\mathrm{ReLU}_{256}\to\mathrm{ReLU}_{256}
    \to\mathrm{ReLU}_{128}\to\mathrm{Output}.
  \label{eq:network}
\end{equation}
The output layer applies softmax to ensure $\sum_i w_{it}=1$.

PPO alternates between rollout collection ($T=252$ trading-day
trajectories) and policy update via the clipped objective:
\begin{equation}
  L^{\mathrm{CLIP}}(\theta)
    = \hat{\mathbb{E}}_t\bigl[\min(r_t(\theta)\hat{A}_t,
      \mathrm{clip}(r_t(\theta),1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon)\hat{A}_t)\bigr],
  \label{eq:ppo_clip}
\end{equation}
with clipping parameter $\varepsilon=0.2$ and GAE advantage estimates.
Training hyperparameters are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:hyperparams}.

\subsection{Neural Network Structural Stability Assessment}
\label{sec:stability_metric}

\subsubsection{Motivation and Formal Justification of Frobenius Distance}

Traditional backtesting metrics evaluate ex-post performance but provide
limited insight into whether learned policies remain structurally stable
under distributional shifts \citep{gu2020empirical}. We use the
normalized Frobenius distance between independently trained networks as
a metric of structural stability, motivated by three considerations.

\textbf{Theoretical grounding.} For two parameter vectors
$\mathbf{w}_a,\mathbf{w}_b\in\mathbb{R}^d$, the Frobenius norm satisfies
$\|\mathbf{w}_a-\mathbf{w}_b\|_2 = 0 \Leftrightarrow \mathbf{w}_a=\mathbf{w}_b$,
providing a geometry-preserving global measure of parameter convergence.
Under mild smoothness conditions on the policy objective, networks with
lower Frobenius distance produce more similar action distributions:
$\|\mathbf{w}_a-\mathbf{w}_b\|_2\leq\delta \Rightarrow
\sup_{s}\mathrm{TV}(\pi_{\mathbf{w}_a}(\cdot|s),\pi_{\mathbf{w}_b}(\cdot|s))\leq
L\delta$, where $L$ is a Lipschitz constant of the policy network and
$\mathrm{TV}$ denotes total variation distance.

\textbf{Sensitivity to spurious fitting.} If EID provides spurious
signal (period-specific rather than persistent), gradient descent on
different non-overlapping subsamples would assign different weights to
the EID channel, increasing Frobenius distance. Conversely, persistent
signal induces consistent gradient directions, reducing distance. This
makes Frobenius distance a natural detector of genuine versus spurious
information.

\textbf{Computation and interpretability.} Unlike kernel methods or
information-theoretic distances, the Frobenius norm requires no density
estimation and scales with network size in a transparent way; the
normalization factor renders it dimensionless.

\begin{remark}[Limitations of Frobenius distance]
Frobenius distance is a necessary but not sufficient condition for
policy quality: two networks can be close in parameter space yet both
learn an economically incorrect allocation rule. We therefore supplement
it with (a) signed gradient attribution (Table~\ref{tab:feature_importance}),
(b) out-of-sample performance net of transaction costs
(Table~\ref{tab:net_performance}), and (c) permutation tests that
destroy EID's economic content while preserving its statistical
properties (Section~\ref{sec:permutation}).
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Metric Definitions}

Let $\{\mathbf{W}\}_{M_a}$ and $\{\mathbf{W}\}_{M_b}$ denote the
ordered collections of trainable weight matrices from two PPO models.
The normalized layer-aggregated distance is:
\begin{equation}
  D_{\mathrm{norm}}(M_a,M_b)
    = \frac{\|\mathbf{w}_a-\mathbf{w}_b\|_2}
           {\tfrac{1}{2}(\|\mathbf{w}_a\|_2+\|\mathbf{w}_b\|_2)},
  \label{eq:frobenius_norm}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{w}_a$ and $\mathbf{w}_b$ stack all comparable network
weights. The denominator is scale-invariant with respect to network
magnitude, preventing artificially inflated distances for networks with
larger parameter scales.

Summary statistics over a set of $K$ partition pairs:
\begin{align}
  \bar{D} &= \binom{K}{2}^{-1}\sum_{i<j}D_{\mathrm{norm}}(M_i,M_j),
  \label{eq:mean_distance} \\
  D_{\max} &= \max_{i,j}D_{\mathrm{norm}}(M_i,M_j),
  \label{eq:max_distance} \\
  \mathrm{CV}_D &= \mathrm{std}(D_{\mathrm{norm}})/\bar{D}.
  \label{eq:cv_distance}
\end{align}

\paragraph{Statistical inference.}
All pairwise distance comparisons are supported by:
(i) Mann--Whitney $U$ tests (non-parametric, appropriate for
non-normal distance distributions);
(ii) bootstrap confidence intervals ($B=1{,}000$ resamples of the
distance matrix); and
(iii) effect sizes (Cohen's $d$ and rank-biserial correlation $r$).

\subsubsection{Subsample Partitioning Strategy}

We partition training data across three dimensions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Temporal}: 4-year rolling windows (2016--2019,
    2018--2021, 2020--2023, 2022--2025) yielding six pairs.
  \item \textbf{Market regime}: Bull vs.\ bear subsamples (split by
    30-day realized return relative to median), yielding one pair.
  \item \textbf{Sector}: High-emission sectors (Manufacturing, Energy,
    Transportation) vs.\ low-emission sectors (Technology, Finance,
    Healthcare), yielding one pair.
\end{itemize}
Each partition pair generates a normalized Frobenius distance.
Averages and distributions are compared across Models A, B, and C.

\subsection{Three Model Variants}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Model A (Baseline PPO)}: Standard PPO without EID
    features. State space: $\{P_t,I_t\}$.
  \item \textbf{Model B (PPO+EID)}: PPO with EID as additional state
    variable. State space: $\{P_t,I_t,E_t^{\mathrm{raw}}\}$.
  \item \textbf{Model C (PPO+EID+Regime)}: Model B with regime-dependent
    reward weighting (Proposition~\ref{prop:regime}).
\end{itemize}

Models are trained ten times with different random seeds to quantify
stochastic training variance separately from cross-regime variance.

\section{Data and Sample Construction}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Environmental Information Disclosure (EID)}

The primary dataset comprises 59,051 firm-year observations of official
EID scores for Chinese A-share listed companies from 2008 to 2025.
Scores come from public databases and listed-company disclosure
documents, including annual environmental reports, sustainability
reports, and regulatory filings. The database reports an aggregate EID
score ranging from 0 to approximately 50, with higher values indicating
more transparent environmental disclosure.

\paragraph{Data integrity rules.}
(1) Scores are used in their public aggregate form; no sub-category
decomposition, PCA reduction, or researcher-defined reweighting is
applied.
(2) Scores are carried forward with calendar alignment; no imputation
of unreported values before disclosure dates.
(3) The EID score is not winsorized or adjusted for industry exposure
in the primary analysis; industry-residualized EID is tested in
Section~\ref{sec:industry_neutral}.

\subsection{Market Data and Risk-Free Rate}

Daily price and volume data are sourced from Chinese Stock Connect, the
Shanghai and Shenzhen Stock Exchanges, Bloomberg Terminal, and Wind
Information Services. Price series are adjusted for splits, dividends,
and corporate actions before return construction. The risk-free rate is
the 1-year Chinese government bond yield from the China Central
Depository and Clearing Company.

\subsection{Sample Selection and Filtering}

Inclusion criteria:
(1) Continuously listed on Shanghai or Shenzhen exchanges with available
daily price data.
(2) Minimum average daily trading volume of CNY\,1,000,000 (liquidity
filter).
(3) Complete daily pricing data for 2016--2024.
(4) EID scores available for at least 3 consecutive years.
(5) Excluding companies in bankruptcy proceedings or under extended
trading suspensions.

The final sample comprises approximately 1,800 stocks. Training period:
2016--2021. Out-of-sample evaluation: 2022--2024.

destroying firm-level information), $\bar{D}$ reverts to the Model A
level ($0.479\pm0.031$), confirming that the stability improvement is
attributable specifically to the economic content of EID rather than to
a dimensionality effect.

Model C achieves a further 21\% reduction (B to C: CI [15\%, 28\%]),
indicating that regime-adaptive reward weighting additionally stabilizes
learned policies by allowing the algorithm to weight environmental
objectives appropriately to market conditions.

\subsection{Mediation Analysis: Empirical Test of the Causal Mechanism}
\label{sec:mechanism_test}

The hypothesized chain (Equation~\ref{eq:mechanism_chain}) involves an
intermediate variable: reward variance $\mathrm{Var}(r_t\mid s_t)$.
We test the mediating role of reward variance through a three-step
procedure.

\textbf{Step 1 (EID $\to$ Reward Variance).} We regress the episode-level
reward standard deviation on portfolio-average EID score across
training episodes:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{SD}(r_t)_{k} = \beta_0 + \beta_1\overline{\mathrm{EID}}_k
    + \beta_2 \mathrm{Regime}_k + u_k,
  \label{eq:step1}
\end{equation}
where $k$ indexes training episodes. Estimated $\hat\beta_1<0$ would
be consistent with EID reducing reward noise.

\textbf{Step 2 (Reward Variance $\to$ Frobenius Distance).} We regress
$D_{\mathrm{norm}}$ on $\widehat{\mathrm{SD}(r_t)}$ from Step 1:
\begin{equation}
  D_{\mathrm{norm},k} = \delta_0 + \delta_1\widehat{\mathrm{SD}(r_t)}_k + v_k.
  \label{eq:step2}
\end{equation}
Estimated $\hat\delta_1>0$ would be consistent with higher reward
variance producing larger parameter distance.

\textbf{Step 3 (EID $\to$ Frobenius Distance, mediated).} The
proportion of the total A$\to$C distance reduction attributable to
the reward-variance channel (versus direct effects of EID on the loss
landscape) is estimated via Baron--Kenny decomposition.

observational data.

\section{Robustness and Out-of-Distribution Tests}
\label{sec:robustness}

\subsection{Permutation Test}
\label{sec:permutation}

The permutation test (reported in Table~\ref{tab:frobenius}) provides
the strongest robustness check. By shuffling EID labels randomly across
firms within each year, we preserve the EID score distribution and the
number of additional state dimensions while destroying firm-level
economic information. Under 500 permutations, $\bar{D}$ reverts to
$0.479\pm0.031$---statistically indistinguishable from Model A
($\bar{D}=0.487$) and significantly above Model B's observed
$\bar{D}=0.312$ ($p<0.002$). This rules out the possibility that
the stability improvement reflects dimensionality expansion rather than
EID's economic content.

\subsection{Alternative Stability Metrics}

To confirm that the finding is not an artifact of the Frobenius
metric choice, we examine three complementary metrics.

\textbf{Spectral distance} (maximum eigenvalue difference of parameter
covariance matrices): Model C achieves 0.156 vs.\ 0.289 for Model A,
confirming that EID aligns the primary directions of parameter variation
across market regimes.

\textbf{Wasserstein distance} (optimal transport cost between parameter
distributions): Model C achieves 0.234 vs.\ 0.487 for Model A,
confirming that EID reduces not only mean divergence but the
distributional spread of parameters across training subsets.

\textbf{Cosine similarity} (scale-invariant alignment of parameter
vectors): Model C achieves 0.82 average cosine similarity across
temporal subsamples vs.\ 0.71 for Model A.

All three metrics tell a qualitatively consistent story: EID
integration substantially improves parameter convergence across
independently trained networks.

\subsection{Industry-Neutral EID}
\label{sec:industry_neutral}

Raw EID may partly capture industry exposure, since technology and
new-energy firms can have systematically higher disclosure profiles than
chemicals or heavy manufacturing firms. We test whether the stability
improvement survives industry-residualization:
$\tilde{e}_{it}^{\mathrm{resid}} = e_{it}^{\mathrm{raw}} - \bar{e}_{g(i),t}$,
where $g(i)$ denotes the industry of firm $i$.

With industry-residualized EID as the additional state variable, $\bar{D}$
for Model B decreases to 0.338 (vs.\ 0.312 for raw EID), confirming
that industry exposure explains part but not all of the stability
improvement. The permutation test remains significant ($p<0.01$) for
residualized EID. We interpret this as evidence that both within-industry
environmental quality variation and cross-industry policy-risk
differentiation contribute to the stability benefit.

\subsection{Hyperparameter Sensitivity}

We conduct a grid search over $\lambda_2\in[0.01,0.25]$,
$\varepsilon\in[0.1,0.3]$, and learning rate $\alpha\in[0.0001,0.001]$.
The stability improvement persists across 95\% of the explored parameter
space. Degradation occurs only at the extremes: $\lambda_2>0.3$
(excessive environmental constraints) or $\varepsilon<0.1$ (overly
restrictive trust regions). The finding that EID reduces Frobenius
distance holding throughout the interior of the hyperparameter space
suggests that it reflects EID's information content rather than
fortuitous hyperparameter tuning.

\subsection{Out-of-Distribution Tests: Policy Shock Events}

\paragraph{2022 regulatory shock.}
During days following major environmental regulation announcements
(emissions trading system expansion, water pollution control
enforcement), Model C achieves lower maximum drawdown and smaller
volatility spikes relative to Model A. This is consistent with
the mechanism that firms with high EID have already internalized
regulatory constraints, making policy announcements less disruptive
to their valuations. We note, however, that these comparisons are
descriptive: the events are not randomized and alternative explanations
(e.g., high-EID firms being larger and more liquid) cannot be ruled out.

\paragraph{Sector-specific shocks.}
During coal production restriction announcements, Models B and C
reduce coal sector exposure more quickly than Model A. We attribute
this to EID-informed learning rather than mechanical rule-following,
but acknowledge that a clean causal test would require an exogenous
shock to EID information availability.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Macroeconomic Interpretation}

Our empirical findings must be situated within China's structural
economic transformation toward ``new quality productive forces.''
Within this framework, EID functions not merely as an ESG metric but
as a critical macroeconomic data element revealing which firms possess
the organizational capacity to adapt to binding environmental constraints.

The dual-carbon targets (peak emissions 2030, carbon neutrality 2060)
and the 15th Five-Year Plan's mandate for mandatory corporate EID create
a regulatory environment where environmental compliance becomes a
differentiating competency. Our finding that firms with superior EID
are associated with lower parameter instability in learned portfolio
policies is \emph{consistent with} the view that transparency about
environmental performance reveals management quality and organizational
flexibility under regulatory transition, though this interpretation
rests on the associational evidence presented.

The regime-adaptive weighting mechanism (Proposition~\ref{prop:regime})
reveals that environmental preferences can reflect rational utility
maximization under resource constraints, not merely ethical luxury:
during capital-abundant periods investors weight green objectives more
heavily, and this endogenous adaptation is grounded in the distribution
of risk budgets across market states.

For long-horizon institutions, the framework is most useful as a
\emph{policy-risk filter} rather than as a short-horizon return
predictor. The practical implication is a disciplined reallocation rule:
when the market enters a stress window, tilting toward firms with
pre-disclosed environmental governance records reduces left-tail exposure
without requiring discretionary judgment.

\subsection{Limitations and Boundaries of Evidence}

\paragraph{Causal identification.}
The central limitation is that we cannot establish the full causal chain
empirically. The mediation analysis (Table~\ref{tab:mediation}) accounts
for only 28\% of the stability improvement through the reward-variance
channel; the remaining mechanism is less precisely identified. A
cleaner test would exploit exogenous variation in EID availability---for
example, a regulatory mandate that forced disclosure by a subset of
firms---but such variation is not available in the current sample.

\paragraph{Gross vs.\ net performance.}
DRL strategies exhibit substantially higher turnover than passive
benchmarks (68\% annually for Model A, 58\% for Model C). At realistic
transaction costs of 0.2--0.3\%, the DRL advantage is reduced but
persists. At higher costs (above $\sim$0.5\%), the advantage over
passive benchmarks would likely disappear. Live trading performance
may also differ from backtest performance due to market impact, data
latency, and implementation shortfall.

\paragraph{China-specific context.}
The EID scoring system, the regulatory environment, and the market
microstructure are specific to Chinese A-shares. Cross-country
generalizability remains untested. The regime-adaptive weighting
mechanism may require recalibration in markets with different
risk-premium dynamics.

\paragraph{Disclosure quality vs.\ environmental impact.}
EID scores capture disclosure quality (transparency, reporting
completeness) rather than actual environmental outcomes (emissions
reductions, compliance performance). Firms that disclose extensively
are not necessarily the most environmentally responsible in terms of
actual impacts. Future work integrating actual emissions data
(Scope 1--3 data from national registries) could sharpen the
policy-risk interpretation.

\paragraph{Single algorithm.}
We test EID integration only within PPO. Whether similar stability
benefits appear in other DRL algorithms (e.g., Soft Actor-Critic,
Twin Delayed DDPG) or in attention-based architectures remains
an open question.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presents three empirical contributions.

\textbf{Methodologically}, we demonstrate that the database-reported
aggregate EID score can be incorporated directly into a PPO state space
without decomposing or reconstructing the core disclosure variable, and
we establish normalized Frobenius distance as a tractable, formally
justified metric for assessing structural stability of learned portfolio
policies.

\textbf{Empirically}, the PPO+EID+Regime framework exhibits 36--50\%
reductions in normalized Frobenius parameter distance across
independently trained models and market regimes (bootstrap 95\% CI:
[29\%, 57\%]; $p<0.001$ by Mann--Whitney $U$; effect size $d=1.84$).
A permutation test confirms this stability improvement reflects EID's
firm-level economic content rather than a dimensionality artifact. A
mediation analysis provides evidence consistent with the hypothesized
mechanism---reward variance reduction mediates approximately 28\% of
the stability improvement---though formal causal identification is
beyond the scope of this observational study.

\textbf{Operationally}, net-return comparisons at three cost levels
(0.1\%, 0.2\%, 0.3\% per turnover unit) show that Model C outperforms
six benchmarks including Markowitz MVP, equal-weight, momentum, risk
parity, a pure-DRL baseline, and a static ESG tilt across all tested
cost levels. At the central cost assumption of 0.2\%, the Sharpe ratio
of 0.58 compares favorably with 0.29 for Markowitz MVP, with maximum
drawdown of $-21.3\%$ vs.\ $-35.3\%$.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Key limitations include: (1) the full causal chain from EID to
gradient stability is not formally identified in the observational
setting; (2) transaction costs reduce but do not eliminate the
performance advantage; (3) the framework is calibrated to Chinese
A-shares and requires additional testing for cross-country
generalizability; and (4) EID scores measure disclosure quality, not
actual emissions or environmental outcomes.

\paragraph{Future work.}
Priority extensions include: (1) integration of actual emissions data
(Scope 1--3) when available from national carbon registries; (2) a
regression discontinuity or difference-in-differences design exploiting
mandatory disclosure policy changes to identify the causal effect of
EID on policy-risk exposure; (3) testing across alternative DRL
algorithms and architectures; (4) cross-country analysis of
regime-adaptive green weighting; and (5) real-time parameter stability
monitoring to trigger automatic strategy retraining under distributional
shift.
